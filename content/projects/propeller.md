## Why I Tried It

I found a [YouTube propeller tutorial](https://youtu.be/9M-JQYerg0M?si=DCDPgmCMy3xgBOys) and followed along because its shape was outside the simple extrudes and fillets I had used so far. This was a learning exercise built from someone else's guidance, not an original propeller design or a performance-tested part.

## Building the Shape

The most useful idea was **projecting 3D geometry** into a new sketch. Once I could carry a reference from one part of the model into another, the blade began to feel less like a shape I had to guess and more like geometry I could control.

I also practiced the **coil**, **sheet/surface extrude**, and **thicken** tools. The sequence helped me understand why a thin surface can be easier to shape first, before turning it into a solid with thickness. The coil introduced a different way to reason about a rotating form.

![Original CAD view of the propeller modeling exercise](/images/projects/propeller/cad-preview.png)

## What Was Difficult

I had to slow down and pay attention to references and feature order. A projected edge is only useful when I understand where it came from and what changes upstream will do to it. The tutorial gave me a route through the model; reproducing a clean result still required me to follow each dependency carefully.

## Result and Next Step

I completed a CAD propeller study and came away with a new surface-modeling workflow. I have not simulated, fabricated, or measured it, so this page makes no claim about aerodynamic performance. The next independent step would be to set my own blade dimensions and test how those decisions change the form.

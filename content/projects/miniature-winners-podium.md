## Why a Podium?

A little Max Verstappen Funko Pop I got for my birthday was standing on my desk at exactly the same level as my earphones. As a big Verstappen and Red Bull fan, that felt wrong. A miniature winner's podium seemed like his rightful place, and a fun excuse to learn more Fusion 360.

I had a rough shape in mind, but I kept adding details that would make me try something new rather than stopping at a plain block.

## Modeling the Details

I gave the steps an angled face to make the shape more playful. In the process I learned Fusion 360's **draft** feature, which lets me add an angle to a surface instead of awkwardly rebuilding the whole form. I also wanted to make it unmistakably Max's, so I put the short “Du Du Du DU” chant on the front. That led me to the **emboss** tool: I made the lettering on an offset plane and brought it onto the face of the model.

![Original CAD view showing the stepped podium, top recesses, and front lettering](/images/projects/pedestal/cad-preview.png)

## The First Slicing Session

The CAD model was only part of what I wanted to learn. I downloaded Bambu Studio, watched a beginner guide, and imported the podium to make a sliced version. I tried different infill patterns and percentages, wall thicknesses, variable wall settings, and supports. I was learning what each option changed in the toolpath, not claiming that I had already found an optimal print recipe.

While I was on a roll, I also sliced my self-locking towel hook. That more complicated shape pushed me to look at tree supports and print orientation, especially around its curved surfaces and internal ball.

## What Is Still Untested

After roughly two hours of modeling and slicer experiments, the podium was ready for a first print. It is still a digital model. I have not checked whether the lettering comes out legibly, whether the podium works at the figurine's scale, or whether my chosen settings produce a clean physical part. My next step is to get practical feedback at Fab Lab Aachen and make that first prototype.

The project was playful, but it gave me a useful habit: every time I thought “that looks fine,” I asked what one more feature could teach me. Draft, emboss, and slicing all came from that question.

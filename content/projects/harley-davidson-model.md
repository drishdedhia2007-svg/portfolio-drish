## Why I Built This

My fascination with engines and the open road eventually led me to
motorcycles — and specifically to cruisers. Harley-Davidsons, in
particular, captivated me: the presence, the power, the sheer aura of
them. Digging through every model, I settled on a favorite: the Sport
Glide. I couldn't build the real thing, obviously — but I could build
a version of it. So I decided to try, using nothing but newspaper.

## My Approach

I'd always loved handicrafts, inspired by shows like *Art Attack* and
*M.A.D.*, and I'd spend hours making small paper creations. This project
was different, it was the first time I designed something entirely
from my own imagination, with no reference video or tutorial to follow.

At 14, that meant learning some real fundamentals on the fly: how to
break a complex shape down into separate parts that could be built
individually and assembled later, how to plan dimensions so the pieces
would actually fit together, and how to reinforce weak points, I used
toothpicks internally where the newspaper alone couldn't hold its
structure.

## Challenges

Working only from imagination, with no plans or references, meant
constant trial and error. Keeping the structure rigid enough to hold
its shape was a recurring problem, and the sheer scale of planning and
assembling every part individually made this a long, demanding process
for a teen working alone.

## Result

A newspaper model of a Harley-Davidson Sport Glide; imperfect, but
entirely my own design, built part by part from scratch.

## What I Learned

This was one of the foundational moments in my path toward mechanical
engineering, even if it didn't look like "engineering" at the time. It
taught me patience, planning, and persistence; how to break a big
idea into buildable pieces, and how to keep going when something isn't
working the first time. More than anything, it was proof that I could
take something I imagined and bring it into the physical world with my
own hands.
